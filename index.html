Reference
Measurement definitions and planning rules of thumb

Radiographic measurements

Reimers Migration Percentage (MP) — On AP pelvis, percentage of femoral head lateral to Perkins line. Hip surveillance threshold: > 30% triggers concern, > 40% commonly indicates reconstruction (CPUP / AACPDM guideline).

Acetabular Index (AI) — Angle between Hilgenreiner line and acetabular roof. > 30° usually indicates a pelvic procedure is needed in addition to VDRO.

Neck-Shaft Angle (NSA) — Coronal-plane angle of femoral neck axis to shaft. Normal child 135-145°; in CP often 150-170° (coxa valga). Goal post-op 100-120°.

Head-Shaft Angle (HSA) — Angle from physeal axis to shaft; valgus capital remodeling indicator (Bauer / Robin).

Anteversion — Anterior tilt of femoral neck. Normal child ~30° decreasing to ~15° adult; CP often 50-70°. CT (Murphy/Reikerås) or clinical (Ryder / trochanteric prominence) test.

VDRO planning rules of thumb

Varus correction = current NSA − target NSA. A lateral closing wedge of that magnitude is removed from the inter/subtrochanteric region.

Derotation = current AV − target AV. Performed by externally rotating distal fragment after the osteotomy.

Blade plate selection — pick a plate angle so the blade sits along the femoral neck axis with the side plate flush on the lateral shaft after correction. For a target NSA of 110°, a 90° blade plate is commonly used.

Predicted MP reduction — estimate ≈ 0.7-1% MP reduction per 1° of varus, capped by acetabular coverage. If AI > 25° or predicted MP > 25%, add a pelvic procedure.
